New Income Idea · Finance
Private Note Lending Through a Self-Directed IRA

The pitch: Open a self-directed IRA and become the bank — lend on real estate notes secured by property, earning interest inside a tax-deferred (or tax-free, if Roth) account instead of owning the property directly.

Why it fits you: You already run Tabbut Real Estate LLC and know how to underwrite a property; note lending uses that same judgment without the landlord workload (no tenants, no maintenance calls), and diversifies your real estate exposure by asset type rather than just adding another rental.

Realistic range: Private notes typically yield 8–12% annually; income scales with capital committed, so $25,000 deployed might return $2,000–$3,000/year, tax-advantaged.

Start here: Directed IRA or a comparable SDIRA custodian to open the account and review promissory-note investing rules.

Friction: Requires an SDIRA custodian (setup + annual fees), due diligence on each note/borrower, illiquidity until the note pays off, and care around prohibited-transaction rules if lending to anyone related to you.
