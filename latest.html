HHS Convenes Experts to Set Standards for Clinical AI

STAT News reports HHS is convening outside experts to help build federal standards for how clinical AI tools get validated and deployed — the regulatory scaffolding question that's been missing while adoption outpaces oversight. It follows a related move a week earlier: CMS signaling intent to revamp how it pays for clinical software and AI, tying reimbursement to demonstrated impact on patient outcomes rather than a flat per-use fee.

Why it matters to a physician-entrepreneur: if you're building or advising on anything AI-adjacent in clinical care (POCUS interpretation tools, triage support), the standards-and-payment framework taking shape now will define what's fundable and billable in 2027.

Free Market Medicine

DPC · Concierge · Policy
📈
DPC · Jul 21
DPC "At an Inflection Point"

Medical Economics reports the DPC market is projected to grow from $70.17B (2025) to $75.11B in 2026 — a 7% CAGR — driven by finalized IRS HR1 rules that now let HSA dollars pay for DPC memberships (capped at $150/month to preserve the statutory line from concierge medicine). Employer-driven direct contracting is now outpacing insurer-led deals, with large purchasers reporting double-digit savings, and 35+ states now define DPC agreements as not insurance.

Autonomy, Not Just Income, Driving the Shift

Medical Economics frames 2026 as a pivotal year for concierge conversion, with burnout, administrative burden, and volume-rewarding reimbursement converging to push physicians toward membership-based models. One physician quoted: the concierge switch "enabled me to reclaim my power, and real joy in caring for patients" — the practice-ownership pitch that resonates most with EM-adjacent specialists eyeing an exit ramp.
