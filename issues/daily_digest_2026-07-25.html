Journal Article
Medetomidine's Arrival in the Drug Supply

Annals of Emergency Medicine published "Emergence of Medetomidine in the Illicit Drug Supply: Implications for Emergency Care and Withdrawal Management" (Lynch et al.). Medetomidine — a veterinary alpha-2 agonist now showing up as a fentanyl adulterant — produces a withdrawal syndrome that doesn't respond to standard opioid protocols and can mimic severe opioid withdrawal with added bradycardia/hypotension. Worth a quick read before your next shift in a high-use-prevalence catchment.

Market Growth
DPC Hits an HSA Inflection Point

The direct primary care market is projected to grow from $70.17B (2025) to $75.11B in 2026 (7% CAGR), now over 2,700 practices nationwide. The bigger unlock: IRS-finalized HR 1 rules now permit HSA dollars to pay DPC membership fees, capped at $150/month to preserve the statutory line between DPC and concierge medicine. Medical Economics calls it an inflection point as employer-direct-contracting starts outpacing insurers on cost and quality.

Doctors Band Together as Medicaid Cuts Bite

KFF Health News reports independent primary care doctors are increasingly forming Independent Physician Associations (IPAs) to negotiate collectively as Medicaid cuts and value-based reimbursement squeeze solo and small practices. It's the market-based counter-move to consolidation — doctors aggregating negotiating leverage without selling out to a hospital system or PE roll-up.

Health Sharing Ministries

🧾
Legislation
A Bill to Make HCSM Payments Tax-Deductible

H.R. 2062, pending in the 119th Congress, would amend the Internal Revenue Code so that health care sharing ministry membership — including shares of medical expenses and administrative fees — counts as a deductible medical expense, the same as premiums. It builds on the earlier Health Care Sharing Ministry Tax Parity Act introduced by Reps. Kelly, Murphy, and Smith.

Congress.gov · 119th Congress Source
💳
HRA Guidance
IRS Opens the Door for HRA-Funded HCSM Payments

A proposed IRS rule would let employer HRAs reimburse employees tax-free for monthly health-sharing-ministry payments, alongside DPC fees — treating both as qualified medical expenses per Take Command Health. Practical impact: roughly a million Americans on HCSM plans could see their employer able to subsidize the share tax-free for the first time.
